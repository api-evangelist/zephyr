This meetup was a wonderful opportunity for attendees to deepen their understanding of Zephyr RTOS, network with peers, and share valuable insights. Below are the highlights from the event, ranging from the technical sessions to the conversations that continued during the networking sessions.</p>
<h3><strong>Venue</strong></h3>
<p>The meetup was held at the Brother Museum Communication Hall in Nagoya. Its convenient access from Nagoya Station attracted participants from all over the country.</p>

<h3><strong>Meetup</strong></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This meetup was held in the Brother Museum, where many sewing machines and other products are on display.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Brother Industries was originally founded as a sewing machine manufacturer. They have evolved into a leader with formidable expertise in embedded systems, and the museum beautifully showcases this long-standing heritage of precision and innovation.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The program included a special tour of the Brother Museum, which helped foster interaction and deeper connections among the attendees. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Additionally, there was a fantastic surprise for on-site participants: Nordic Semiconductor generously provided the nRF54L15 DK—their next-generation ultra-low-power platform—free of charge. This offered a perfect opportunity for attendees to try out Zephyr RTOS on cutting-edge hardware.</span></p>

	<h3>Nordic Semiconductor Invited Talk – John Kenney</h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The first session was an invited talk delivered online by John Kenney from Nordic Semiconductor. He discussed the company’s goals for low-power wireless communication and sustainability. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A key highlight was their approach to Edge AI and device management cloud solutions. He explained how their NPU-equipped products and &#8220;Neuton AI&#8221;—an ultra-compact AI model that runs in just 5KB—can significantly reduce latency and power consumption. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">He also introduced the benefits of nRF Cloud, a powerful management platform that enables device observability and OTA updates post-deployment. <a href="https://www.zephyrproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/1_john_Nordic-Semi-Japan.pdf">Slides here</a>.</span></p>

	<h3>Recent Arduino Trends: Uno Q and ArduinoCore-zephyr – @soburi</h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">@soburi provided a technical deep dive into the integration of the Arduino platform with Zephyr RTOS. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">He shared the background of Qualcomm’s acquisition of the Arduino project and introduced the architecture of the new Zephyr-based &#8220;Arduino Uno Q&#8221; board.  </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">The session covered advanced topics such as utilizing Zephyr&#8217;s dynamic loading (LLEXT) to run Arduino APIs and using the DeviceTree &#8220;Nexus&#8221; feature to resolve compatibility issues with unique pin assignments. <a href="https://www.zephyrproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2_soburi_Arduino-Zephyr.pdf">Slides here</a>.</span></p>
